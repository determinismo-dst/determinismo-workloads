5. ASSUMPTIONS
======================================================================
Round 3's NA1–NA8 stand (NA1: an nREPL eval runs clojure.main/repl over the code; NA2:
load-file is Compiler.load; NA3: *ns* user per connection; ...).
New:
- NA9. The spec's equivalent of jank's `(catch cpp/jank.runtime.object_ref e …)` is Clojure's
  catch of what `throw` threw (ExceptionInfo); runtime errors (C++ exceptions in jank) are not
  caught by it on either side. `:default` is read as ClojureScript's catch-all binding the value
  (only the noted observation depends on it).
- EA4-1. nREPL's `eval` fields `line`/`column` set Clojure's reader position (nREPL's documented
  meaning: "the line/column number in [file] at which [code] starts").
- EA4-2. Clojure's LineNumberReader folds "\r\n", "\r" and "\n" into one terminator (its
  javadoc).
- EA4-3. nREPL's completions `:ns` of a var candidate is the var's namespace (E2).
- EA4-4. `jank repl` with piped stdin reads with std::getline; interactive isocline returns one
  line per Enter (E4 is stated for piped stdin).
- EA4-6. The spec's analysis is all-or-nothing (round 2's analyzer), while Clojure's DefExpr
  interns a var before analyzing its init and keeps it when a later analysis fails. On a spec
  analysis failure the defs met in preorder before the first symbol that resolves to nothing
  (not a var, special form, `cpp/` name or a name bound anywhere in the form) are interned. jank's
  side is exact (the analyzer replica keeps what it interned).
- EA4-5. Knuth estimates are averages over 300 random paths with mixed seeds; they bound
  feasibility, not exact sizes.
