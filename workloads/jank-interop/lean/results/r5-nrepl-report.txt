1. DEVIATIONS
======================================================================
D1 (more precise than R128; not new as a class). The interrupt op.
  Program (in-process, as round 4's u41): clone, then
    (handle-message {"op" "interrupt" "id" "i" "session" <the clone's>})
  nREPL 1.8.0 (measured, results/r5-nrepl-interrupt-jvm.txt): {:status #{"session-idle" "done"}}.
  jank: {:status [:error :unknown-op :done] :op "interrupt"} (handler.jank 5-8: no :interrupt
  method; describe.jank 7-15 does not list it).
  Every interrupt jank receives gets unknown-op: without a session (nREPL:
  #{error session-ephemeral done}), an unknown session (#{error unknown-session done}), an idle
  session (#{session-idle done}), a running eval with another interrupt-id (#{error
  interrupt-id-mismatch done}), a running eval with its id or none (#{done}, and the eval gets
  #{interrupted done}).
  Ordering (core.jank handle-client 79-106): on the eval's connection the interrupt is read only
  after the eval returns, so its reply follows the eval's value and done; nREPL handles each request
  on a pool thread as it arrives (server.clj handle 39-48) and answers the interrupt while the eval
  runs. With an eval that never returns, jank never answers the interrupt (nor the eval): the
  connection's only thread is inside the eval. On another connection jank answers unknown-op at once
  and the eval runs on (sessions are global in nREPL: one process-wide atom, session.clj 20, looked
  up by id in `session` 311-350, so its interrupt reaches the eval from any connection; measured, its
  #{interrupted done} for the eval then arrives on the interrupting connection).
  World I census (results/r5-nrepl-interrupt.txt): 72 cases (eval ends / never returns × interrupt
  sent right after it, after its done, on a second connection, with no eval × session the eval's /
  absent / unknown × interrupt-id absent / the eval's / another). jank's outcome is one of nREPL's
  only in the 9 cases where the interrupt is never sent (after the done of an eval that never ends);
  in 54 the interrupt gets unknown-op where nREPL gives its status (the eval's own replies are one of
  nREPL's outcomes); in 9 (sent right after an eval that never returns) jank never answers the
  interrupt, which nREPL answers in every race. jank's side of the 36 cases whose eval ends rerun
  through round 3's World T conn replica (requests as raw bytes): 1,535,724 schedules within 2
  preemptions, every one ends as the code reading says, no World T property broken.
  Tests: vD1-interrupt-of-an-idle-session, vD1b-interrupt-without-a-session (.r5-scratch/nrepl/
  tests.jank; jank only: they call jank's handler namespace; nREPL's side is the JVM measurement).
  The hang (interrupt on a diverging eval's connection) has no test form: shell steps, with jank's
  nREPL on port P:
    (printf 'd2:op5:clone2:id1:ce'; sleep 1; printf 'd2:op4:eval2:id1:e4:code17:(loop [] (recur))e';
     sleep 1; printf 'd2:op9:interrupt2:id1:i12:interrupt-id1:ee'; sleep 10) | nc 127.0.0.1 P
  nREPL 1.8.0 answers i at once (#{error session-ephemeral done}, as the request names no session; with
  the clone's session added, #{done}, and e gets #{interrupted done}); jank answers the clone only:
  neither e nor i ever gets a reply, and the connection's thread spins in the loop.
  Status: R128 ("no interrupt; one connection is strictly sequential") made precise: the replies,
  their order, and the unanswered interrupt. Confidence: high (the handler is 8 lines; the loop is
  read; World T agrees).

No new deviation from T7: the reduced world finds nothing (section 7), and T7's verdict is now
complete at the bounds below.

2. PROOFS
======================================================================
T7 (round 3's World T, one client, partial reads, every op; ≈3.2·10¹¹ timelines at ≤1 preemption,
round 4's Knuth estimate, rerun here: mean 320,945,244,760 over 300 paths).

(a) The chunking, proved for every input (Chunk.lean).
  readStep/readLoop replicate read-message (core.jank 58-70) over round 2's `parse` replica.
  ★ chunking_independent: for every byte string m whose proper prefixes all parse to nil and which
  parses whole to [v nil], and EVERY chunking of m (any number of parts, empty ones allowed), the loop
  gives exactly v, once, at the part that completes m, and ends with an empty buffer; one whole read
  gives ([v], none). (Lemmas readLoop_prefix, readLoop_empty.)
  ★ chunking_independent_all: the same for every message of a list satisfying `hypsHold`.
(b) T7's six requests satisfy the hypotheses (ParseK.lean). Round 2's parser is well-founded
  recursion, which neither `decide` nor the kernel evaluates; ParseK.lean copies it as structural
  recursion with a fuel argument (recK/listK/dictK), and
  ★ k_agrees proves, for every fuel, input, depth, accumulator and position, that the copy either runs
  out of fuel or gives exactly the original's result; ★ parseK_sound lifts it to `parse`.
  t7_k evaluates the copy in the kernel on all 261 proper prefixes and the 6 whole requests, both
  `char` signednesses; t7_hyps turns that into the hypotheses for round 2's own parse, and
  ★ t7_chunking_independent: for every chunking of each of T7's requests, read-message's loop gives
  the message a whole read gives, once, with an empty buffer.
(c) The world-level reduction (Reduce.lean, header): a partial read that leaves a request incomplete,
  and the `.top none` step after it, are stutter steps of the abstraction α (a connection's unread
  bytes back in its inbox); the completing read leaves the state a whole read leaves; no other
  thread reads a connection's remainder or inbox; the judge and the deadlock detail are α-invariant;
  deleting stutter steps (and the client's no-op blocked steps after them) from a schedule gives a
  schedule of the whole-read world with no more preemptions. Hence T7's verdicts at ≤ b preemptions
  are those of the whole-read world at ≤ b.
  - Steps 1-2 checked by execution of round 3's own tConnStep at every reachable pre-read state of
    the whole-read world, every split point j (bytes already buffered) and every next chunk length n
    (stutterCheck): at ≤1 preemption (5,312 timelines) and at ≤2
    (218,400 timelines): 6 distinct pre-read states (one per request: every schedule reaches each
    read in the same state), 13,227 runs of tConnStep, every one as claimed
    (results/r5-nrepl-run2.txt, run3.txt).
  - Cross-check on T7 itself: round 3's T7 world itself (partial reads on), walked completely over
    every timeline with at most one partial read: ≤0 preemptions 16,384 timelines, ≤1 preemption
    1,392,512 timelines (results/r5-nrepl-cuts1.txt); nothing, and every timeline ends in the one
    final state of the whole-read world. (The walk skips a subtree as soon as a second partial read
    is drawn: 13,857 and 996,600 such runs.)
  - Steps 3-5 are an argument from Threads.lean's code (tClientStep, tAcceptStep, tJudge, tStuck,
    interleavePB), not mechanized: the sdk has no notion of independence or of a process-level
    simulation to state them in, and the World T state (closures in the jank world JW) has no
    decidable equality to model-check a quotient with.
(d) The whole-read world run to completion: ≤0 preemptions 64 timelines, ≤1 5,312, ≤2 218,400, all
  complete, nothing (no deadlock, every request done, replies well-formed, :out and values as the
  spec's). One more reduction, from the code: round 3's tConnStep draws `net.read.eintr` every time a
  read finds the inbox empty, even with eintr off, because Lean lifts the `(← decideRare …)` out of
  `m.cfg.eintr && (← decideRare …)` (Threads.lean 251); with eintr false its value is never used, so
  timelines differing only there are the same timeline. Holding it at 0: ≤2 complete 3,335 (the same
  verdict as the 218,400 above), ≤3 complete 89,835, ≤4 stopped at 688,909 (800 s; its resume path is in
  results/r5-nrepl-run5.txt). Nothing in any.
  So T7 at ≤1 and ≤2 preemptions, which rounds 3 and 4 stopped at 1,500,000 and 1,489,000 of
  ≈3.2·10¹¹ and ≈1.2·10¹³, is decided (nothing), and so is ≤3, modulo steps 3-5.
  Every timeline at every budget run ends in the same final state (one fingerprint: replies, evals,
  fds, temp files, sockets, the jank world's shape): the one-client world is confluent at those bounds,
  as the commutation argument says it is at every bound (the client only reads whole replies the
  connection appended; the connection never reads the client's state).

Where neither worked: the world-level steps 3-5 are not a Lean theorem (above). No other T scenario
was reduced: two clients (T1-T6, T8-T10) share fd 1 and the jank world, so their steps do not commute.

5. ASSUMPTIONS
======================================================================
Round 3's NA1-NA9 and round 4's EA4-1..EA4-6 stand.
- nREPLA5-1. nREPL 1.8.0 (the latest release on Clojars when read) is the interrupt op's spec, with its

- nREPLA5-3. T7's world-level reduction (Reduce.lean header, steps 3-5) as argued from the code.
- nREPLA5-4. `net.read.eintr` is unused when eintr is off (Threads.lean 251, read above), so holding it
  at 0 loses no timeline.
- nREPLA5-5. A chunked run stays within round 3's 6,000-round step bound (a run past it is reported as
  "the timeline ends within the step bound"; none was, in the cut walks).
Round 3 model note (not a change to its verdicts): every World T tree is inflated by a factor of 2 per
read that blocks, from the unused `net.read.eintr` draw; round 3's and 4's T-world counts and Knuth
estimates include it.

8. THEOREMS (★ = for every input)
======================================================================
Chunk.lean:
- ★ chunking_independent (every message satisfying the hypotheses, every chunking): read-message's
  loop yields exactly the message, once, with an empty buffer; a whole read gives the same.
- ★ readLoop_prefix (the invariant), ★ readLoop_empty (empty chunks change nothing).
- ★ chunking_independent_all (every message of a list for which hypsHold is true).
- prefixesNone_spec, isNoneP_iff, parse_none (helpers).
ParseK.lean:
- ★ k_agrees: for every fuel, input, depth, accumulator and position, recK/listK/dictK either run out
  of fuel or give exactly what round 2's parseRecur/parseList/parseDict give (by induction on the
  fuel; recK_succ/parseRecur_succ etc. identify both with one body; bodyR_mono, bodyL_mono,
  bodyD_mono, agrees_bind).
- ★ parseK_sound: a run of the copy that does not end in .crash is round 2's parse.
- t7_k (kernel evaluation, decide +kernel): T7's requests, both signednesses, every prefix nil,
  every request whole.
- t7_hyps, t7_chunking_independent: for every chunking of each of T7's requests, the loop gives the
  whole read's message, as theorems about round 2's parser.
Axioms (results/r5-nrepl-axioms.txt): each of the nine main theorems depends on propext,
Classical.choice and Quot.sound only. No sorry, no native_decide (t7_k is `decide +kernel`).

======================================================================
9. LIBRARY GAPS
======================================================================
- Preemption-bounded scheduling with an observer (interleavePBObs) to collect reachable states.
- No decidable equality on World T states: fingerprints (strings) stand in for state equality.
- Well-founded recursion does not evaluate in the kernel: a fuelled structural copy, with a proof that
  it agrees whenever it does not run out of fuel (ParseK.lean), turns finite checks into theorems.
- A pruned walk (timelines with at most k partial reads, subtrees past the bound skipped).
