V38 (also V39, V40, V45): #inst is read through std::chrono::parse with four formats
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Change: inst_from_string is replaced by a direct reader of clojure.instant's grammar and checks
(instant.clj 50-51 parse-timestamp, 139-156 validated):

    yyyy[-MM[-dd[Thh[:mm[:ss[.fraction]]]]]][Z|(+|-)hh:mm]

- It matches as Clojure's regex does: the most fields after which an offset ends the text, so
  "2020-05:00" is year 2020 at -05:00.
- Fraction digits past 9 are dropped (zero-fill-right).
- The validated ranges: month, day per month and leap year, hour, minute, second ≤ 60 only at minute
  59, offset ≤ 23:59.
- A leap second rolls into the next minute, as the lenient GregorianCalendar does.
- The instant is sys_days + h:m:s - offset, then the nanoseconds.
- Text outside the grammar is "Unrecognized date/time syntax: …", thrown as an object (unchanged).
- New: an instant outside the 64-bit nanosecond range of jank's time point (the years 1678-2261) is a
  read error. Before, that conversion could overflow silently.
- It no longer builds std::locale("en_US.utf-8") per read, so #inst no longer depends on that locale
  being installed (round 5's coreA5-1).
- The libc++ branch is untouched. The new reader needs no std::chrono::parse, so #inst could work on
  macOS too; that is left out of this diff.
