2. PROOFS
======================================================================
(a) ★ eintr_draw_unused (Theorems.lean). Round 3's tConnStep (Threads.lean 251) draws net.read.eintr on
  every read that finds the inbox empty and open, even with EINTR off, because Lean lifts the
  `(← decideRare …)` out of `m.cfg.eintr && …`. For EVERY connection, remainder, instruction tail, eval
  state, bindings and process state with that remainder parsing to nil, the socket's inbox empty and not
  closed, and EINTR off: whatever the draw gives, the step returns `.blocked` and the process's memory
  and threads are unchanged — or the timeline ends at its decision limit (`.limit`; lemma
  next_error_limit: the sdk's Decisions.next fails only with `.limit`). Round 5's nREPLA5-4 is now a
  theorem; holding the draw at 0 (round 5's `whole0` walks, the "eintr 0" counts here) loses nothing.
  Also checked at every reachable state of T7 and T7w: 34 (state, thread) steps draw it, both values
  lead to the same state (results/r6-nrepl-t7.txt).
(b) The log ordering (Graph.lean byCid/orderMem/canonMem), for every input:
  ★ byCid_bucket: for every log, every client c ≤ n and either order, client c's part of the ordered
    log is its part of the original log, same entries in the same order.
  ★ byCid_append: ordering, appending, ordering = ordering the appended log (appends commute with the
    ordering, so a log built by any interleaving of per-client appends orders to one canonical log).
  ★ byCid_filter_sub, byCid_find?_sub, byCid_any_sub: any filter / find? / any whose predicate keeps
    only one client's entries gives the same result on the ordered log.
  ★ The World T readers themselves, for every state and either order (orderMem d):
    tRepliesOf_orderMem (Threads.lean 547–548, the judge's replies of a request; the client's `.wait`
    step filters the same way, 512), evalsFind_orderMem (tJudge 587), handlerErrorsFind_orderMem
    (tJudge 597), hungAny_orderMem (tConnStep 356).
  Lemmas: bucket_unique, bucket_of_key, filter_flatMap_bucket, cidsOf_spec, flatMap_congr'.
  Not proved: the cross-client order in tJudge's loop over `sent` (it picks WHICH violation a timeline
  reports first, never whether it has one) and in tStuck's detail text; and that the steps write the
  logs only by appending (read from Threads.lean 231, 309, 358, 422, 483, 506). Those are checked at
  every step taken (section 8: 0 differences over 8,708,758 step runs and final states).
(c) What did not work, and why.
  - A proof that the state-graph counter (Graph.lean countFrom) equals the sdk walk over interleavePB:
    interleavePB is a `for` loop in ProcessM with mutable locals, and countFrom a partial memoized
    recursion; relating them needs a loop invariant over EStateM that the sdk does not provide. Instead
    the counter reproduced EVERY complete walk count of rounds 3–5 exactly: 17 timeline counts and 4
    violation counts (results/r6-nrepl-validate.txt: T1, T2, T4, T5, T6, T8, T9, T10, T7 three ways).
  - A proof that the derived BEq is identity (LawfulBEq) for the 30-odd state types: not attempted;
    derived structural BEq on plain data compares every field (NV's Float by its bits).
  - A proof of round 5's reduction (α a stuttering simulation) for every input: the steps are not
    small enough to reason about in Lean (tConnStep is over 200 lines over EStateM); it is checked on the
    complete reachable set instead (section 8), which decides T7, the only world it was for.

12. MODEL ERRORS OF EARLIER ROUNDS CORRECTED
======================================================================
- T7's size. Rounds 4–5 estimated T7 at ≈3.2·10¹¹ timelines at ≤1 and ≈1.2·10¹³ at ≤2 (Knuth's
  estimator, Seg.lean). Exact: 1.25·10⁸¹ at ≤1, 2.1·10⁸³ at ≤2. Cause: Seg.estimate draws each decision
  BY WEIGHT (the sdk's random source: net.read.split weights [6, 1]), while Knuth's estimator is unbiased
  only for uniform choices; partial reads (the huge subtrees) were drawn 1 time in 7. Even ≤0 has
  2²⁵⁵·64 timelines (every chunking of 261 bytes). The whole-read estimates (no weighted split) were
  unbiased but too spread: T7w ≤2 6,201 vs 218,400, ≤3 78,705 vs 5,934,304; T3 ≤1 12,236 (round 4) vs
  1,358,848; T9 ≤2 10,414 (round 4) vs 21,112,352; round 3's "~10M" for T9 ≤2 vs 21,112,352.
- Where the earlier walks stopped, as fractions now known: round 5's T7w ≤3 (5,774,800 of 5,934,304,
  97%), T7w eintr-0 ≤4 (688,909 of 1,805,188, 38%); round 4's T3 ≤1 (1,206,000 of 1,358,848, 89%);
  round 3's T9 ≤2 (853,000 of 21,112,352, 4%); round 3/4's T7 ≤1 and ≤2 (1.5 million of 1.25·10⁸¹ and 2.1·10⁸³).
- Round 5's "World T's states have no decidable equality" (Reduce.lean, report section 9): they are plain
  data with derivable structural equality (StateEq.lean).
- Round 5's α (Reduce.lean header): "a connection's .top none shown as .readMsg" is true only after an
  incomplete read (a nonempty remainder); as written it also rewrote a new connection's first
  instruction and the empty remainder an end-of-file read leaves. The check found exactly those
  (2 states, 4 steps) before the definition was made precise; no verdict changes.
- Re-read "agrees" verdicts resting on an assumption (rule 5): T3's and T9's "nothing" rested on the
  capture lock (fixCapture, R125 set aside). On jank's real capture, with only R125's two properties set
  aside, T9 ≤3 and T3 (section 8) still find nothing: the lock hid R125 and nothing else. Round 3's other
  set-asides are known deviations (R111, R112, R127, R131, R136, R138, S71–S74, S76).
