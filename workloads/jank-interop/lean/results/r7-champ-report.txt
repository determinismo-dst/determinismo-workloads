======================================================================
Over round 3's replica of immer's champ.hpp (Jank/R3/Immer/Champ.lean) and round 6's slot view.

(a) Law 1 without the invariant (Jank/R7/Champ/LawOne.lean).
- ★ add_walk: for EVERY well-formed node (round 3's wfNode; no hash-path invariant, no uniqueness),
  do_add of v leaves find? of a query's predicate over the walk its hash takes unchanged, given only
  that v is not Equal to the query, a value Equal to v is not Equal to the query, and a value Equal
  to the query hashes as the query. Every branch of do_add (collision replace/insert, child,
  replace value, make_merged, insert value).
- ★ get_add_other_wf: so law 1 for add (assoc/conj) holds for every well-formed champ under a lawful
  equality. Round 6's get_add_other assumed Inv (HP and uniqueness); neither is needed.
- ★ sub_walk, ★ get_sub_other_hp: law 1 for sub (dissoc/disj) holds for every well-formed champ
  meeting HP (uniqueness and the size premise dropped): every branch of do_sub, including the
  singleton passed up and inlined by an ancestor (the inlined value is on its own hash's walk, by
  round 6's walk_complete).
- law1_sub_fails_without_HP (decide +kernel, with badChamp_wf, badChamp_get_before/after,
  badChamp_not_HP): HP cannot be dropped for sub. Over Nat with == and the identity hash (natLawful:
  a lawful equality), a well-formed champ holding 225 and 289 in the root's child at bit 1 (225 at
  bit 3 though its hash names bit 7): get 225 is none; after sub 289 the two-value node collapses,
  225 is passed up and inlined at the root, and get 225 finds it.
- Can a jank program build such a champ? No. HP holds of champ::empty() and is kept by do_add,
  do_sub, do_add_mut and do_sub_mut for every hash function and equality (round 6: add_step,
  sub_step, addMut_step, subMut_step), so a champ off its hash paths needs a stored object whose
  to_hash changed while stored. jank's value types are immutable (collections cache their hash;
  lazy seqs realize once); atoms, volatiles, transients, arrays, delays, futures and promises
  override neither equal nor to_hash (their headers), so they use object::equal (identity,
  object.cpp 47-50) and object::to_hash (the address, 69-72), and Boehm's collector does not move
  objects. So: law 1 holds for every champ jank builds under a lawful equality, and for add under
  any well-formed champ; the sub counterexample is outside jank.

(b) The transient sequence theorem (Jank/R7/Champ/Trans.lean).
- ★ doSubMut_found: do_sub_mut of a key get finds is never "nothing", and at the root it is a tree,
  on the mutating and the copying branch alike, for every hash and equality (round 5 had do_sub only).
- ★ subMut_cases: sub_mut either leaves the champ (key absent: round 5's subMut_absent) or removes one
  value Equal to the key with size - 1 (SubOut from round 6's subMut_step).
- ★ inv_addMut, ★ inv_subMut, ★ keys_addMut, ★ keys_subMut, ★ get_addMut_other, ★ get_subMut_other:
  Inv, law 2's step and law 1 for assoc!/conj! and dissoc!/disj! under a lawful equality (through
  shared lemmas ★ inv_of_add, ★ keys_of_add, ★ get_other_of_add, ★ inv_of_sub, ★ keys_of_sub,
  ★ get_other_of_sub, which also cover the persistent steps).
- ★ buildT_keys, ★ buildT_from_empty: law 2 for EVERY sequence mixing persistent and transient steps
  (assoc/conj, dissoc/disj, assoc!/conj!, dissoc!/disj!, any owner tokens), from any champ meeting
  Inv and from champ::empty(): Inv is kept (each key once, count = the iteration's length) and the
  keys are exactly Clojure's model (present iff the last step naming an = key added it). transient
  and persistent! change only the owner token and move the champ (round 3's Champ.transient,
  Transient.persistentL/R), so a jank program's conj/disj/transient/conj!/disj!/persistent! chain on
  one collection is such a sequence.
- ★ modelT_persist, ★ buildT_same_keys: a sequence and the same one with every transient step made
  persistent yield the same keys (transients agree with persistent operations by contents).

Not proved:

4. WHAT IS NOT EXACT
======================================================================
- Clojure's side is a set/map model (Util.equiv: identity, then =), not Clojure's HAMT with hasheq.
  It assumes Clojure's hasheq agrees with its = on the pool (true by Clojure's design for these
  kinds: Numbers.hasheq strips BigDecimal zeros, hashes -0.0 as 0.0 and long-range BigInts as longs;
  ASeq/LazySeq/StringSeq/MapEntry hash ordered). Checked on Clojure 1.12.3 only through the six test
  programs.
- Each pool value is one object (as through a fn parameter); identity is pool-index equality. A
  NaN read twice would be two objects.
- jank's collections inside the pool (sets, maps) compare elements with round 1's containsH
  abstraction ("same full hash and equal"), not a CHAMP walk; only flat elements are CHAMP-walked.
- The walk threads one collection: no transient kept alive beside its persistent result (round 3/4
  checked aliasing separately). transient/persistent! as in round 3's replica.
- hash-map of several entries is assumed to insert them in argument order (create_from_seq);
  H7-1's hash-map form gives nil in either order.
- Hash values of keywords, longs and BigDecimals are the earlier rounds' replicas (not observed on
  jank); H7-1 and H7-2 depend on them (their controls test the premise on jank).
- Clojure's transient set conj replaces the stored map VALUE (ATransientSet.conj -> impl.assoc(o, o))
  while keeping the key; only contains? and seq (keys) are observed, so this is not modeled.
