M9-11. A hash set's or hash map's seq order. New.
- (vec (hash-set 5 9 8)): [9 5 8]; [8 9 5]. (str (hash-set 5 9 8)): "#{9 5 8}"; "#{8 9 5}".

  [0 7 1 4 6 3 2 10 8 11 9 5].
- control: (vec (hash-set 5 8)): [5 8] in both.
- Why: both index the 32-bit hash 5 bits at a time from the low end, and a long hashes alike in both
  (Murmur3 hashLong). immer's CHAMP node keeps inline values apart from sub-nodes and champ_iterator

  then its children; Clojure's BitmapIndexedNode interleaves entries and sub-nodes by position. So a
  node with a sub-node at position i and an entry at j > i lists the entry first in jank, after the
  sub-node's keys in Clojure. 5 and 9 share position 23, 8 is alone at 31.
- Reach: seq, keys, vals, first, printing, reduce, vec, into, = of seqs: every hash set (any size)
  and every hash map past 8 entries whose keys crowd a node position before a later lone key. Of

- Path: obj/persistent_hash_set.cpp create_from_seq 45-53, to_string 83-86;
  obj/detail/base_persistent_map_sequence; immer champ_iterator. Clojure: PersistentHashMap.java
  (BitmapIndexedNode, ArrayNode, NodeSeq), PersistentHashSet over it.

  past the int range for doubles (Java saturates); not used by any test.

  object type is read from the headers (obj_behaviors), not replicated.
- The assert and demo models compare messages as character lists; pr-str of the form is taken as given.
